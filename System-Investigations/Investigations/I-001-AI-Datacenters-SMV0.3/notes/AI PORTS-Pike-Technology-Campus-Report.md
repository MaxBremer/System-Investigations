# PORTS-Pike Technology Campus — Pike County, Ohio Site Report

**As of August 19, 2026**

## Bottom line

PORTS-Pike is no longer merely a speculative SoftBank power-and-land announcement. OpenAI has signed for approximately **8 IT-GW** under a **20-year lease**, SB Energy will build, own and operate the data-center campus, and NVIDIA has become the exclusive compute-platform provider. NVIDIA has also supplied the unusual bankability mechanism: residual-value guarantees capped at **$105 billion** for the initial **4.25 IT-GW**, plus a **$1.5 billion equity investment in SB Energy**.

It is nevertheless an early-development megaproject, not an operating campus. A ceremonial groundbreaking took place in March, a wetlands/water-quality application was filed in May, the project entered the federal FAST-41 permitting process in June, and the first **800 MW** is targeted for 2028. No data-center capacity is publicly confirmed under vertical construction or energized. OpenAI itself says later phases depend on financing, permits, environmental reviews, new power plants and new transmission.

The project is best treated as **Stargate in commercial substance, but not yet explicitly branded as a Stargate site in its August launch materials**. OpenAI's January 2026 preferred partnership with SB Energy was expressly announced “as part of Stargate,” and PORTS-Pike uses that same developer, tenant and investment platform. However, the PORTS-Pike releases use the campus name rather than the Stargate label. This report therefore includes it in the investigation without pretending the naming question is settled.

The power program is as exceptional as the compute commitment: **10 GW of new generation**, including at least **9.2 GW of natural-gas generation backed by $33.3 billion of Japanese funding**, plus **$4.2 billion** of AEP Ohio transmission work. On the newest and cleanest denominator—8 IT-GW—those two disclosed power layers equal **$4.69 billion per IT-GW**, before the undisclosed cost of the remaining 0.8 GW of generation, the data-center shell/MEP, land and remediation, compute hardware, financing costs or refreshes.

## Does it count as Stargate?

There are two defensible answers, depending on whether “Stargate” means a formal project label or the OpenAI–SoftBank infrastructure program.

### Formally labeled: not yet

The August 17 OpenAI, NVIDIA and SB Energy releases call the project **PORTS-Pike Technology Campus**. None of the launch materials reviewed explicitly says “PORTS-Pike is a Stargate site.” That differs from OpenAI's five-site announcement, which deliberately attached the Stargate name to the listed projects.

### Economically and organizationally: probably yes

OpenAI's January agreement with SoftBank and SB Energy was explicitly described as part of Stargate. It created a non-exclusive preferred development partnership, funded SB Energy with **$500 million each from OpenAI and SoftBank**, and positioned SB Energy to build OpenAI campuses and associated power infrastructure. PORTS-Pike is exactly that structure at much larger scale: SB Energy is the owner/developer, OpenAI is the sole announced tenant, SoftBank is the sponsor, and NVIDIA is supplying and credit-supporting the compute platform.

The cleanest classification is therefore: **include PORTS-Pike in the Stargate capital-stack analysis, but preserve a “not formally branded” footnote until OpenAI or SoftBank applies the name directly.**

Source: [OpenAI–SB Energy Stargate partnership](https://openai.com/index/stargate-sb-energy-partnership/).

## What is actually being built

The public record describes one integrated program spread across **remediated federal land at the former Portsmouth Gaseous Diffusion Plant and adjacent private land**:

1. A phased AI data-center campus delivering approximately 8 IT-GW to OpenAI.
2. Ten GW of new grid-connected generation, including at least 9.2 GW of natural gas.
3. AEP Ohio transmission expansion including new 765 kV lines and four substations.
4. An interstate natural-gas pipeline to support the generation fleet.
5. Water, road, electrical and site-remediation work across federal and private parcels.

Despite the legacy “Portsmouth” name, the campus is near **Piketon in Pike County**. It is unrelated to the Lordstown/Trumbull County manufacturing project and should not be merged with it.

Earlier federal and project descriptions repeatedly call it a **“10 GW data center.”** The August 17 commercial agreements make the electrical boundary clearer: OpenAI is contracting for **8 GW of IT load**, while the energy program totals **10 GW**. Those numbers are not contradictory if the larger figure includes cooling, power conversion, network and other facility overhead, reserve margin, and generation made available to the grid.

The ratio is **1.25 GW of generation per IT-GW**. It should not be labeled a PUE of 1.25: DOE says excess generation and transmission capacity may serve the grid, and the final operating dispatch and redundancy design are undisclosed.

## Land, remediation and the unresolved site plan

The phrase “at the former Portsmouth plant” hides a two-land-base project.

- **Federal land:** DOE owns the former enrichment site and is leasing remediated land to an SB Energy affiliate. DOE says SB Energy will pay for accelerated cleanup and remediation. A DOE project account identified **189 acres** for the initial phase, but the lease term, rent, exact parcel boundaries and cleanup payment are not public.
- **Private land:** A May 28 Section 401 application by **SE DC DevCo, LLC**, an SB Energy affiliate, describes a 10 GW complex across approximately **1,016 acres** at 1003 Bobo Road. The filing says the developer has options on parcels held principally by Woodburn Heights LLC, Green Eyes LLC and Roe Farms LLC.
- **Final split:** SB Energy told WOUB that the federal and private sites together total 10 GW and that the allocation between them is not final. The permit application includes alternative layouts rather than a selected plan.

This split changes both execution and public-finance analysis. Federal land remains government-owned and is generally outside local property-tax treatment; private parcels could generate property tax or receive abatements. No complete lease economics, purchase-option prices, local abatement package or remediation budget has been disclosed.

Sources: [Ohio Section 401 application](https://woub.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/SB-Energy-application.pdf), [WOUB land report](https://woub.org/2026/06/15/company-plan-massive-data-center-complex-pike-county-includes-private-land/), and [DOE fact sheet](https://www.energy.gov/articles/fact-sheet-department-energy-ensuring-affordable-energy-access-ohio-while-powering-future).

OpenAI will start paying only when completed capacity becomes available. NVIDIA's guarantee likewise applies only after the lessor meets the ready-for-service conditions for the relevant leased premises. These contractual gates reduce payment-before-delivery risk, but they also mean the announced lease and guarantee are not evidence that the whole campus has been financed or constructed.

The strongest current classification is: **development and permitting active; site plan evolving; initial phase targeted for 2028; zero operational capacity; full buildout conditional.**

## Power: a funded concept, not yet a permitted fleet

DOE and SB Energy describe a power program of unusual scale:

- **10 GW total new generation.**
- **At least 9.2 GW natural gas**, leaving up to 0.8 GW of other generation whose technology and cost are not disclosed.
- **$33.3 billion of Japanese funding** for the gas portion under the U.S.–Japan Strategic Trade and Investment Agreement.
- **$4.2 billion** of customer-funded AEP Ohio transmission, including new 765 kV facilities and four substations.
- A dedicated data-center rate structure intended to make the project pay whether it uses the reserved power or not.
- The ability to reduce campus load or provide backup power to the grid in emergencies.

The PORTS-Pike website says the new gas generation will be **owned by the U.S. Government** and funded in part through the U.S.–Japan agreement. SB Energy is the developer/builder, but no public document reviewed explains the generation SPVs, government ownership mechanics, Japanese capital instrument, operator, power-purchase terms, return allocation or asset disposition. “$33.3 billion in funding” should therefore not be simplified to SB Energy equity or a conventional federal grant.

AEP's first publicly described line is the **Baku–Gavin project**, about 50 miles of new 765 kV transmission through Pike, Jackson and Gallia counties. AEP says the customer will pay for the required transmission investment and that the project still requires Ohio Power Siting Board approval. The broader $4.2 billion program is larger than this one line; its project-by-project scope and schedule remain incomplete.

The engineering record is still preliminary. The reviewed public file identifies a Section 401 application and FAST-41 process, while OpenAI explicitly conditions later development on permits and environmental reviews. No final air permit, complete gas-plant configuration, pipeline capacity agreement or full interconnection study was found. For 9.2 GW of gas generation, those are not secondary details; they are the critical path.

Sources: [DOE fact sheet](https://www.energy.gov/articles/fact-sheet-department-energy-ensuring-affordable-energy-access-ohio-while-powering-future), [DOE partnership announcement](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-partnership-ensure-affordable-energy-and-power-americas-ai), [PORTS-Pike project site](https://portscampus.com/), and [AEP Piketon Area Improvements](https://www.aeptransmission.com/ohio/PiketonArea/).

### Fuel and carbon scale check—not a permit forecast

If the entire 9.2 GW gas fleet averaged 90% output, it would generate **72.53 TWh/year**. At an illustrative heat rate of 7,000–8,000 Btu/kWh, it would consume roughly **508–580 million MMBtu**, or about **478–546 billion cubic feet of Ohio-quality natural gas per year**. Direct combustion emissions would be approximately **26.9–30.8 million metric tons of CO2 per year**.

That is a scenario, not an operating forecast. The plant technology, heat rate, capacity factor, grid dispatch, carbon controls and final gas capacity are unknown. It is included to show why the air permit and interstate pipeline cannot be treated as routine follow-on paperwork. The inputs use EIA's modern combined-cycle efficiency range and 2025 Ohio gas heat content, plus EPA's **53.06 kg CO2/MMBtu** stationary-combustion factor.

Sources: [EIA gas-generation efficiency](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=61444), [EIA natural-gas heat content](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/ng/ng_cons_heat_a_epg0_vgth_btucf_a.htm), and [EPA emissions factors](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P101AWWT.TXT).

## Cooling and water

OpenAI says the data centers will use **closed-loop, air-cooled systems** that recirculate water rather than cooling towers that continuously evaporate it. After the initial fill, OpenAI expects ongoing water use to resemble an office complex with a comparable workforce—restrooms, sinks, washdown, maintenance and landscaping—rather than an evaporatively cooled hyperscale campus.

That is a meaningful design disclosure, but not yet a water budget. OpenAI has not published initial fill volume, maximum makeup demand, design WUE, source capacity, treatment chemistry, wastewater discharge or drought case. The plan is to use DOE's existing onsite water system and fund new community water infrastructure; expected consumption will be published after final design.

The historic enrichment plant's water use is not a valid baseline for concluding the new campus is low impact. The relevant tests are the final gallons per day, withdrawal source, initial-fill timing, community-system upgrades and enforceable permit limits.

Source: [OpenAI project announcement](https://openai.com/index/openai-joins-ports-pike-project/).

## The NVIDIA guarantee: what it is and is not

NVIDIA's August 17 Form 8-K removes much of the ambiguity around the largest number in the announcement.

- NVIDIA entered multiple **residual-value guarantees** covering leases for approximately 4.25 IT-GW.
- Aggregate payment exposure is capped at **$105 billion** for the initial commitment.
- Each guarantee generally becomes effective only when the relevant lease commences and ready-for-service conditions are satisfied, expected beginning in 2028.
- A trigger requires OpenAI insolvency/default or failure to pay.
- NVIDIA generally covers the shortfall between the lease's guaranteed minimum value and what SB Energy recovers by reletting or selling the asset.
- NVIDIA can assume the lease, direct a reletting or sale, permit termination, or temporarily defer remedies while paying specified project costs.
- OpenAI must reimburse and indemnify NVIDIA for amounts NVIDIA actually pays.
- The support can terminate early if OpenAI achieves a satisfactory credit rating, among other events.

The cap equals **$24.71 billion per initially guaranteed IT-GW**, but that ratio measures contingent maximum exposure, not construction cost. It cannot be added to the $10 billion first phase, $33.3 billion gas program or $4.2 billion transmission budget. Economically, the guarantee substitutes NVIDIA's credit behind an unrated tenant and should lower the lessor's financing risk; its actual reduction in interest rate or required equity has not been disclosed.

Source: [NVIDIA Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000069/nvda-20260817.htm).

The first-phase ratio is the only public shell/campus-like figure, but it may contain front-loaded common works. A straight-line extrapolation would imply $100 billion for 8 IT-GW, which is not a disclosed project budget and is too sensitive to phasing to use as a base case.

### Abilene comparability scenario—not a PORTS-Pike estimate

The prior Abilene work used an indicative compute-hardware envelope of approximately **$33.3 billion per IT-GW**. Applying that ratio mechanically to 8 IT-GW yields about **$266 billion of compute hardware**. That is not a forecast: PORTS-Pike will use future NVIDIA DSX generations, and pricing, power density, procurement discounts, networking architecture and refresh cadence may be very different.

The scenario is useful for one reason. Even the disclosed **$37.5 billion gas-and-transmission program** could be much smaller than the installed compute fleet. The largest missing cost bucket remains GPUs/CPUs/networking, not real estate.

## Financing and public support

The financing architecture is partly visible but not closed end to end:

1. **Platform equity:** OpenAI and SoftBank invested $500 million each in SB Energy in January. Ares had separately provided $800 million of redeemable preferred equity. NVIDIA is now adding $1.5 billion. These are platform investments and must not be summed as PORTS-Pike project equity.
2. **Tenant cash flow:** OpenAI signs a 20-year lease and pays only as capacity becomes available. It says payments will come from business revenue, cash flow and investor capital.
3. **Credit substitution:** NVIDIA's residual guarantees backstop initial lease economics, allowing lenders to underwrite closer to NVIDIA-supported residual value than to OpenAI's standalone credit.
4. **Project debt:** Reuters reports that the eventual structure is expected to combine equity with project-finance loans and possibly public bonds. Amounts, lenders and terms are not yet defined.
5. **Sovereign-linked power capital:** Japanese funding supports the gas build, while the project site says the resulting generation will be U.S.-government-owned. The exact instrument is undisclosed.
6. **Utility infrastructure:** SB Energy pays the $4.2 billion AEP program through a dedicated customer arrangement rather than socializing the cost to ordinary ratepayers.
7. **Federal land and cleanup:** DOE supplies a leasehold on remediated federal land; SB Energy pays for accelerated cleanup. The value of that land access and the tax treatment are not quantified.

This is a more credible financing story than a headline $500 billion corporate promise, but it is still conditional. The NVIDIA guarantee becomes effective phase by phase, the project debt is not fully specified, and OpenAI says development depends on financing and permits. The guarantee improves bankability; it does not eliminate completion, demand, technology-obsolescence, power-price or refinancing risk.

Sources: [OpenAI–SB Energy partnership](https://openai.com/index/stargate-sb-energy-partnership/), [NVIDIA SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000069/nvda-20260817.htm), and [Reuters financing report](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/nvidia-invest-15-billion-sb-energy-under-openai-data-center-deal-2026-08-17/).

## Jobs, taxes and community benefits

OpenAI forecasts **35,000 construction jobs** over the buildout and **2,500 long-term operating jobs**. Earlier first-phase statements described roughly 4,000 construction jobs and 300–400 operating roles. These numbers may include peak, direct, indirect or project-supported employment; no annual full-time-equivalent schedule is public.

The community fund now totals **$80 million**, with equal $40 million commitments from SB Energy and OpenAI. OpenAI also promises annual public reporting on local hiring, community investment, water use and project energy use, and says the parties will develop an Ohio Community Compact. These future reports are unusually useful potential audit points.

OpenAI expects hundreds of millions of dollars in state and local tax revenue over the project life, but no present-value calculation, property split or incentive schedule is available. Federal land is a distinct tax base from private acreage, and private-property abatements have not been disclosed. OpenAI's separate offer of up to **$84 million in Codex credits** to Ohio students is a product benefit, not construction capital or cash community funding, and should be accounted for separately.

## What is known, inferred and still missing

### High confidence

- OpenAI has contracted for approximately 8 IT-GW under a 20-year lease.
- SB Energy will build, own and operate the data-center facilities.
- NVIDIA is the exclusive AI compute-platform provider and guarantees initial lease residual value up to a $105 billion cap.
- The first 800 MW is targeted for 2028; no capacity is publicly operational.
- The power plan includes 10 GW of generation, at least 9.2 GW of gas, and $4.2 billion of transmission work.
- The final layout spans federal and private land and is not settled.

### Medium-confidence interpretation

- PORTS-Pike belongs inside the Stargate economic program even though the launch releases do not formally use the brand.
- The 10 GW generation / 8 IT-GW ratio provides facility overhead and grid flexibility, but is not a disclosed PUE.
- NVIDIA's guarantee is the key mechanism allowing SB Energy to raise long-duration project debt against OpenAI's lease.
- The reported $10 billion first phase likely includes front-loaded or shared site infrastructure and should not be linearly extrapolated.

### Critical unknowns

- Final federal/private site layout and land economics.
- Full shell/MEP capex, building count, floor area and phase MW.
- Debt/equity structure, lenders, rates, tenor, covenants and financing close.
- Gas-plant technology, number/location of plants, air permits, pipeline and interconnection rights.
- Technology and funding for the remaining 0.8 GW of generation.
- Hardware owner, chip generation, GPU count, network design, capex and refresh cycle.
- Lease rent, power-price formula, escalators, termination rights and service-level tests.
- Numeric PUE, WUE, initial water fill and wastewater limits.
- Federal lease term, cleanup budget, environmental liability and tax treatment.
- Net public subsidy after land, tax, grid, cleanup and community payments.

## Falsifiers and next evidence to watch

- **NVIDIA's next 10-Q:** the guarantee forms should expose more of the minimum-value and remedy mechanics.
- **Project-financing close:** debt amount, pricing and equity would turn the lease-supported concept into a real capital stack.
- **Ohio EPA air permits and gas-pipeline filing:** essential evidence that 9.2 GW of gas can be built and fueled.
- **OPSB certificate and AEP construction notices:** would establish routes, substations, cost recovery and delivery dates.
- **Final Section 401/404 decisions:** would reveal the selected private-land layout and wetland impacts.
- **DOE lease and remediation documents:** would price the federal-land advantage and allocate cleanup liability.
- **First 800 MW notice to proceed, building permits or visible grading:** would distinguish ceremonial groundbreaking from construction.
- **Hardware procurement disclosure:** required for a defensible full-system $/GW rather than an LPS-only number.
- **OpenAI annual community report:** promised source for actual energy, water and local hiring data.
- **Explicit Stargate branding:** would close the classification question without inference.

## Assessment

PORTS-Pike is the clearest expression yet of Stargate's likely mature financing model: **a specialized infrastructure owner holds the land/power/shell assets; OpenAI signs the long lease; a highly rated hardware vendor backstops residual value; project lenders fund the build; and separate sovereign/utility structures finance power.** It is both more bankable and more circular than a conventional hyperscale lease, because OpenAI is simultaneously SB Energy investor and tenant while NVIDIA is supplier, investor and contingent guarantor.

The site's scale is credible as a commercial ambition but not yet as a construction fact. The anchor lease and SEC-filed guarantee materially improve the evidence, yet the land plan is unresolved, most of the power fleet is unpermitted in the public record, and full shell and hardware budgets remain unknown. The honest present conclusion is therefore: **8 IT-GW contracted, 4.25 IT-GW credit-supported, 800 MW targeted for 2028, 0 MW operational, and the majority of physical and financial execution still ahead.**

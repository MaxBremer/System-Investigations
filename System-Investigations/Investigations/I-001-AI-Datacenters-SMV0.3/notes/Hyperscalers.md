## Definition
A hyperscaler is effectively just a cloud computing provider on a large scale. While there's no universal standard, large scale is required in order to host and support large-scale infrastructure and demand.

The name comes from "hyperscale computing", a "method of processing data that allows for software architecture to scale and grow as increased demand is added to the system."

---

## Why It Exists
Most of these cloud-based compute projects (AI, Internet of Things, web apps, whatever) require infrastructure that can scale quickly, both as new products grow 

---

## Example
There are a couple common examples of hyperscalers: Amazon Web Services is the biggest, with Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure not too far behind. While there are smaller ones that exist, scale is obviously a big part of this game; hard to beat the big dogs.

---

## Why It Matters
This is common vocabulary when referring to players in the AI/datacenter space, so understanding it is critical. Hyperscalers already use substantial data center resources for existing operations, even those that don't have heavy investments in their own AI programs; they'll obviously be involved in datacenter buildout.

# Stargate — Shackelford County (Frontier) Site Report

**As of August 17, 2026**

## Bottom line

Frontier is real, financed enough to have started, and far more legible than most Stargate announcements—but it is not operational. Vantage is developing a ten-building, 3.7-million-square-foot campus in Shackelford County for Oracle, which in turn is supplying OpenAI under Stargate. Vantage advertises **1.4 GW of critical IT load** and **more than $25 billion of campus investment**. Texas construction registrations itemize only **$10.565 billion for the ten data-center buildings** plus a **$74 million office/support project**, or **$10.639 billion registered in total**.

That produces the key hard-number split:

- **Headline campus budget:** at least **$17.86 billion/GW**.
- **Registered building and support work:** about **$7.60 billion/GW**.
- **Unreconciled difference:** at least **$14.36 billion**, or **$10.26 billion/GW**.

The difference is not a discovered cost category. It is what remains after subtracting filing estimates from the headline budget. It could contain onsite generation, electrical distribution, land, financing costs, owner contingency, procurement outside the building permits, later scope, or simply different accounting boundaries. Vantage has not published a line-item reconciliation.

The most important execution issue is power. Oracle says the campus will rely on its own gas-fired microgrid. Public permitting reported for the initial generation phase covers roughly **700 MW**, only half the advertised **1.4 GW IT load** before accounting for cooling and other overhead. The public record therefore does not yet show a complete full-campus power solution.

## Evidence labels

- **Disclosed:** stated by a company or government record.
- **Registered:** a project value or schedule in a Texas filing; not an audited final cost or proof of completion.
- **Derived:** arithmetic using disclosed numbers.
- **Scenario:** a comparability calculation, not a site disclosure.
- **Unknown:** no reliable public number found.

## What is actually being built

Frontier is a Vantage campus whose state filings use the confusing project name **“Vantage DC Abilene – TX3 – Project Frontier.”** The filing address uses Abilene, but the county field is Shackelford. It is not the existing Lancium/Crusoe Stargate campus in Taylor County.

The commercial chain is:

1. **Vantage Data Centers** develops and operates the physical campus through ten building-specific Texas LLCs, TX301 through TX310.
2. **Oracle America Cloud Services** is the registered qualifying occupant for all ten buildings.
3. **OpenAI** is the compute customer under the OpenAI–Oracle Stargate capacity arrangement.
4. **Kiewit** is the design firm listed in the Texas building registrations.

This structure matters. The **$25 billion Vantage number is best treated as a developer-side campus investment envelope**, not as the full cost of deployed AI compute. It may include some equipment beyond concrete and mechanical systems, but no source establishes that it includes the GPU fleet Oracle will install and monetize.

## Current state and schedule

Construction is active, but the public schedule has moved or was originally defined too loosely.

- Vantage announced in August 2025 that the first building would deliver in the second half of 2026.
- The ceremonial groundbreaking occurred in December 2025.
- Satellite analysis published by Epoch AI showed roofing underway on the first visible building as of April 14, 2026 and assessed **zero operational capacity**.
- The June 2026 Texas filings put completion of the earliest wave in **2027**, with later buildings extending through **2028**.
- No public notice of first power, substantial completion, Oracle cloud service, or OpenAI compute deployment was found by August 17, 2026.

The most defensible interpretation is therefore: **site work and multiple buildings are in progress, first useful capacity has slipped beyond the original H2 2026 language or “delivery” meant a narrower construction milestone, and full build-out remains a 2028-class target.** The registrations are plans, not proof that every listed building has broken ground.

Sources: [Vantage announcement](https://vantage-dc.com/news/vantage-data-centers-unveils-plans-for-frontier-a-25b-mega-campus-in-texas-to-meet-unprecedented-ai-demand/), [groundbreaking report](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/vantage-breaks-ground-on-texas-gigawatt-data-center-campus-for-openai/), and [Epoch AI site survey](https://epoch.ai/publications/openai-stargate-where-the-us-sites-stand).

## Power: the unresolved full-load bridge

Oracle says Frontier will rely on an onsite power system using “ultra low emission” reciprocating engines. Reporting from public air-permit documents describes an initial configuration of **210 Jenbacher natural-gas engines—197 prime units and 13 backup units—totaling roughly 700 MW**. A later TCEQ notice identifies permit application **182467 / PSDTX1692 / GHGPSDTX266** for the Shackelford facility.

Three caveats break through the marketing language:

1. **700 MW of generation is not 700 MW of IT load.** Cooling, power conversion, networking and auxiliary equipment create overhead.
2. **The first disclosed generation phase cannot serve 1.4 GW of critical IT by itself.** Even before overhead, it is only half the advertised IT capacity.
3. **“98% lower emissions” claims associated with VoltaGrid refer to criteria pollutants, not a 98% reduction in carbon dioxide.** Gas combustion remains the energy source.

Vantage and VoltaGrid separately announced more than 1 GW of gas generation across Vantage's North American portfolio, and VoltaGrid later ordered 1.5 GW of Jenbacher engines for deployments through 2028. Those announcements support the general architecture but do not allocate enough capacity specifically to Frontier to close the gap.

The hard unknowns are the final installed generation MW, fuel-delivery capacity, redundancy margin, grid interconnection role, fuel-price exposure, and whether later phases use additional gas, grid imports, storage, or some combination.

Sources: [Oracle project facts](https://www.oracle.com/data-centers/shackelford-county/), [TCEQ permit notice](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/permitting/air/bilingual/pending-permit-notices/182467-nori-english.pdf), [reported first-phase configuration](https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-building-natural-gas-microgrid-at-new-texas-data-center-2025-10), [Vantage–VoltaGrid partnership](https://voltagrid.com/vantage-data-centers-and-voltagrid-establish-partnership-to-deploy-more-than-one-gigawatt-of-power-generation), and [VoltaGrid engine order](https://voltagrid.com/voltagrid-orders1-5-gw-for-behind-the-meter-power-generation-from-innio).

## Cooling and water

Vantage describes combined air cooling and liquid-to-liquid cooling, with a closed-loop chilled-water system and rack densities above 250 kW. Oracle is more specific: a **one-time-fill, closed-loop, non-evaporative liquid-cooling system**. That design should sharply reduce operational water consumption relative to evaporative cooling, but neither company publishes a design WUE, initial fill volume, water treatment requirement, or maximum makeup-water use. “Near-zero” water use is therefore a design claim, not an auditable site result.

At 90% average utilization, 1.4 GW of IT load alone consumes **11.04 TWh/year**. Actual campus energy input would be higher once PUE is included. Every 0.1 increase in PUE at full IT load adds roughly **1.10 TWh/year** at this utilization.

### Abilene comparability scenario—not a Frontier estimate

Our prior Abilene work used an indicative compute-hardware envelope of roughly **$33.3 billion/GW**. Applying that ratio mechanically to Frontier yields about **$46.6 billion** of compute hardware. Adding the $25 billion campus headline would produce roughly **$71.6 billion**, or **$51.2 billion/GW**, before financing costs and refreshes.

This is deliberately a scenario, not a forecast. It could be wrong by tens of billions because Frontier may use a different chip generation, network design, utilization plan, procurement discount, and hardware/facility accounting boundary. Its value is to show why a $25 billion “data center” headline is not the same thing as a full AI-system cost.

## Financing and public support

Reuters, citing the Financial Times, reported in August 2025 that JPMorgan and MUFG were **in talks** to underwrite a **$22 billion loan**, while Vantage owners Silver Lake and DigitalBridge would contribute **$3 billion of equity**. If that exact structure closed, it would imply **88% debt / 12% equity** against a $25 billion budget. No reliable public closing announcement was found, so it remains a proposed financing structure rather than a confirmed capital stack.

Texas has registered TX301–TX310 as ten **qualifying large data center projects**, each with Oracle as occupant and a Vantage SPV as owner/operator. The program requires at least 250,000 square feet, $500 million of investment, and 40 qualifying jobs; qualifying equipment and electricity can receive a **20-year sales-and-use-tax exemption**. Registration is strong evidence of entity structure and tax eligibility, but it does not prove the 1.4 GW is energized.

This tax benefit is separate from county property tax. Oracle forecasts **more than $100 million of county tax revenue**, but does not publish the period, assessed-value assumptions, or gross-versus-net comparison. The net public subsidy cannot therefore be calculated from available records.

Sources: [reported debt and equity talks](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/jpmorgan-mufg-near-22-billion-data-center-financing-deal-texas-ft-reports-2025-08-20/), [Texas project registry](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/data-centers/data-center-lists.php), and [Texas exemption rules](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/data-centers/).

## What is known, inferred, and still missing

### High confidence

- Vantage is building a ten-building campus; Oracle is the registered occupant.
- The advertised capacity is 1.4 GW of critical IT load.
- Construction is underway and no capacity is publicly confirmed operational.
- The building filings total $10.565 billion and the broader headline exceeds $25 billion.
- The campus's primary near-term power architecture is onsite natural-gas generation.

### Medium-confidence interpretation

- First usable capacity is now more likely a 2027 event than the original H2 2026 target.
- The $25 billion figure is primarily a powered-campus/developer budget rather than a complete GPU-inclusive system budget.
- The initial 700 MW generation phase is a bridge to later power additions, not the final design.

### Critical unknowns

- Final full-campus generation capacity and grid-import rights.
- Confirmed debt closing, interest rate, tenor, covenants, and Oracle lease coverage.
- IT MW assigned to each building and the actual energization sequence.
- GPU vendor, model, count, networking architecture, and hardware capex.
- Contracted gas price, transportation capacity, heat rate, and annual fuel burn.
- Numeric PUE, WUE, carbon intensity, and air-emissions limits at steady state.
- Net public subsidy after state sales-tax exemption and local property-tax payments.

## Falsifiers and next evidence to watch

- **First energization or Oracle region announcement:** would replace the current zero-operational-capacity assessment.
- **A full-site TCEQ permit or disclosed generation order above roughly 1.7–2.0 GW:** would close part of the power gap implied by 1.4 GW IT plus overhead and redundancy.
- **Financing close documents:** would turn the reported 88/12 structure from proposal into fact.
- **Updated TDLR completion dates or certificate-of-occupancy records:** would confirm whether 2027 is the real first-service window.
- **Hardware procurement disclosures:** would make a defensible full-stack $/GW calculation possible.
- **Gas pipeline/interconnection filings:** would test whether onsite generation can run at the advertised scale rather than merely cover the first phase.

## Assessment

Frontier is the clearest example of Stargate becoming a **leveraged, tenant-backed industrial infrastructure program** rather than a single corporate construction project. Vantage bears the powered-campus development role, Oracle is the formal occupant and cloud intermediary, and OpenAI is the demand anchor. The $25 billion headline is credible as a broad campus envelope because it matches the reported debt-plus-equity financing proposal, but the public filings explain only about **43%** of it. Power, not land or building shells, is the central unresolved constraint—and the disclosed first generation phase is visibly too small for the end-state claim.

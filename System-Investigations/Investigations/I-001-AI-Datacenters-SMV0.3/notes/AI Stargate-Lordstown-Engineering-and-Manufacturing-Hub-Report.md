# AI Stargate — Lordstown, Ohio Engineering Data Center and Manufacturing Hub

**As of:** August 19, 2026  
**Evidence convention:** **Disclosed** = stated by a project party or government record; **Registered** = contract, permit, transaction, or official meeting record; **Derived** = arithmetic from disclosed inputs; **Scenario** = sensitivity, not a forecast; **Unknown** = not publicly established.

## Bottom line

Lordstown is widely listed as a Stargate “data-center site,” but the hard evidence does **not** support treating it as a conventional hyperscale compute campus. It is two related projects at the former GM/Foxconn auto plant:

1. a **small engineering/proof-of-concept data center** being built in a former parking area to test chips, racks, and integrated systems for customers; and
2. a **6.0–6.2 million square foot manufacturing and logistics hub** being retooled to mass-produce standardized data-center modules, skids, containers, racks, cabling, networking, and power/cooling assemblies.

That distinction is not semantic. In an April 6, 2026 Lordstown Council meeting, Foxconn Ohio Vice President Shane Brown said the permitted engineering center was the **only** data center on the Foxconn property and described it as “the small one in the parking lot.” He explicitly denied that a full-scale data center was being built inside the plant. Most of the visible capital work—substation replacement, wastewater plant, rail, loading docks, storage pads, roof/fire-system rehabilitation, and shop retooling—supports manufacturing.

OpenAI’s September 2025 announcement said Lordstown and the Milam County, Texas site could **together scale to 1.5 GW** over 18 months. It did not allocate MW between them. The Milam lease separately supports up to 1.2 GW. Subtracting the two produces a residual 0.3 GW, but that is only an inference and conflicts with the later, more specific local description of Lordstown as a small test center. A Lordstown per-GW cost stack is therefore **not defensible**.

The financeable facts are industrial rather than hyperscale:

- SoftBank’s affiliate acquired the plant and machinery from Foxconn for **$375 million**—$88M for land/buildings and $287M for equipment.
- SoftBank and Foxconn formed a **50/50 operating venture** for data-center equipment manufacturing; SoftBank owns the real estate/equipment and Foxconn remains the operator/occupant.
- Reporting has placed possible retooling investment at **up to $3 billion**, but no public project budget, sources-and-uses, debt package, tenant lease, OpenAI capacity contract, or utility agreement substantiates that number.
- OpenAI and Foxconn’s later manufacturing collaboration explicitly created **no purchase commitment or financial obligation**. OpenAI receives early access and an option to buy; the factory is not publicly backstopped by a take-or-pay order book.

The correct project classification is **Stargate supply-chain and engineering-validation node**, not **disclosed gigawatt compute node**.

## 1. Why Lordstown has been misclassified

OpenAI announced in September 2025 that SoftBank had broken ground in Lordstown on an “advanced data center design” targeted for operation in 2026. In the same paragraph, OpenAI said the Lordstown and Milam County sites could **scale to 1.5 GW over 18 months**. Media summaries often compressed those two claims into “a 1.5 GW Lordstown data center.” That is not what the announcement says.

The local record resolves the ambiguity. At the April 6, 2026 Council meeting, Foxconn’s Shane Brown described:

- a fenced, graded area along Hallock Young Road for an **approved engineering data center**;
- a facility intended to demonstrate and test new chips and components for customers;
- mass retooling of the former assembly, stamping, and body-shop areas to produce modular data-center components;
- a replacement substation and new transformers/switchgear;
- a smaller replacement wastewater-treatment plant;
- loading-dock extensions, a concrete component-storage pad, and rail rehabilitation; and
- roof and fire-riser rehabilitation.

When questioned about other “Lordstown data center” hiring and proposals, Brown said that from Foxconn’s standpoint **the only data center was the small engineering center in the parking lot**. He said no full-scale data center was being developed at the Foxconn site.

That statement is more site-specific and later than the broad launch announcement. The most coherent reading is that OpenAI’s “advanced design” refers to a proof-of-concept/reference deployment that validates modular systems before they are manufactured at volume for other Stargate sites.

Primary sources: [OpenAI’s September 2025 site announcement](https://openai.com/index/five-new-stargate-sites/) and the [official April 6, 2026 Lordstown Council transcript](https://www.lordstown.com/agendas/attachment/april-6-2026-2/).

## 2. What is physically under construction

### 2.1 Engineering data center

Preparation began in fall 2025 on the northwest/front side of the property near Hallock Young Road, including excavation of former parking area and removal of part of a solar array. The engineering center has a civil/building permit and site grading was underway by April 2026.

The engineering center’s function is product validation:

- install new chips and components;
- demonstrate an integrated data-center design to potential customers;
- test compatibility, performance, and assembly; and
- feed lessons back into standardized factory-built modules.

No reviewed record discloses its square footage, rack count, IT MW, utility MW, accelerator vendor, commissioning date, PUE, WUE, cooling technology, generator fleet, or capital cost. “Operational in 2026” remains a target, not a confirmed commissioning event.

### 2.2 Manufacturing conversion

The former GM plant is the dominant physical asset. Foxconn described a production model based on **standardized, plug-and-play data-center components** that can be fabricated and shipped to deployment sites. The exact product mix is evolving, but public company statements include racks, cabling, networking, and power and cooling systems.

Plant work described in the April record includes:

- clearing and reconfiguring assembly, stamping, and body-shop areas;
- concrete pads for container/component storage;
- loading-dock extensions;
- rail rehabilitation for outbound shipments;
- replacement of aging fire risers and roof work;
- a replacement industrial substation roughly 100 yards west of the old one;
- new switchgear and transformers; and
- replacement of the degraded wastewater plant with a smaller modern unit, about one-quarter of the present physical size.

Tree clearing and stormwater work were also underway. Brown described one verified stormwater pond and two proposed additions, plus mitigation work involving **26 wetland areas**.

### 2.3 Workforce state

In April, Brown said approximately **250 Foxconn employees** were at home while continuing to receive full pay and benefits, with recalls expected as equipment and work arrived. He did not provide a stabilized production headcount or new-job commitment. That makes old auto-plant employment figures and broad Stargate job claims poor proxies for the current project.

## 3. Ownership, financing, and commercial structure

### 3.1 Asset purchase

On August 4, 2025, Foxconn subsidiaries agreed to sell the property to **Crescent Dune LLC**, identified in subsequent reporting as a SoftBank affiliate:

- **$88 million** for land and buildings; and
- **$287 million** for machinery and equipment;
- **$375 million total**.

Foxconn had acquired the plant from Lordstown Motors in 2022 for approximately $230 million. The 2025 transaction moved real-estate/equipment ownership to SoftBank’s side while preserving Foxconn as occupant and industrial operator.

At approximately 6.04 million square feet, the purchase equates to:

- **~$62 per square foot** on the $375M total; and
- **~$14.60 per square foot** on the $88M land/building price.

These are acquisition ratios for a legacy industrial property, not data-center construction costs.

Source: [Reuters on the $375M plant sale](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/foxconn-agrees-375-mln-lordstown-plant-sale-pivot-towards-data-centres-2025-08-04/).

### 3.2 Operating venture

SoftBank and Foxconn disclosed a **50/50 joint venture** to manufacture data-center equipment at Lordstown. Public descriptions indicate SoftBank’s affiliate owns the real estate and equipment while Foxconn contributes manufacturing operations and remains at the facility under a long-term arrangement.

The exact venture agreement is not public. Missing terms include:

- capital-contribution commitments;
- lease/rent and expense allocation;
- purchase-price adjustments;
- working-capital and inventory financing;
- product-margin allocation;
- minimum-volume commitments;
- governance/deadlock provisions; and
- liability for environmental remediation and legacy plant systems.

Sources: [Reuters on the manufacturing partnership](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/foxconn-softbank-make-data-centre-equipment-ohio-stargate-project-2025-08-18/) and [Manufacturing Dive’s ownership summary](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/foxconn-softbank-joint-data-center-equipment-factory-apple-ai-lordstown-ohio/757995/).

### 3.3 The reported $3 billion retooling number

Reporting in November 2025 said SoftBank could invest up to **$3 billion** to retool the site. No public filing reviewed here breaks that into building rehabilitation, manufacturing machinery, working capital, engineering-center compute, grid/water upgrades, or inventory.

If spread across the full 6.04M sq ft, $3B equals approximately **$497 per square foot**. That arithmetic is useful as a scale check but not as an approved budget. “Up to” could describe a multi-year ceiling rather than committed capital.

Source: [Reuters on the reported investment plan](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/softbank-invest-3-billion-ohio-factory-openai-data-center-information-reports-2025-11-20/).

### 3.4 OpenAI is not a committed offtaker in the published agreement

OpenAI and Foxconn announced collaboration on the design and U.S. manufacture of racks, cabling, networking systems, and power/cooling infrastructure. The agreement gives OpenAI early access and the option to evaluate and purchase systems.

The announcement explicitly says it creates **no purchase commitments or financial obligations**. Therefore:

- there is no disclosed take-or-pay contract;
- there is no disclosed minimum factory utilization;
- OpenAI’s name should not be treated as an order backlog; and
- manufacturing demand risk remains with the venture unless separate, undisclosed contracts exist.

Sources: [OpenAI–Foxconn collaboration announcement](https://openai.com/index/openai-and-foxconn-collaborate/) and [Foxconn’s announcement](https://www.foxconn.com/en-us/press-center/press-releases/latest-news/1905).

### 3.5 What is not publicly financed

Unlike Saline Township, no public record reviewed here shows:

- site-specific project-finance debt;
- a rated bond issue;
- a long-term hyperscale tenant lease;
- an OpenAI/Oracle capacity commitment;
- a power-supply agreement with a minimum bill;
- a tax-abatement certificate tied to a disclosed investment basis; or
- lender completion tests tied to data-center MW.

The visible structure resembles a corporate/JV-funded industrial conversion. SoftBank owns the capital asset; Foxconn operates manufacturing; the engineering center supports product development. That can be economically significant without being a lease-backed compute campus.

## 4. Power, cooling, and water

### 4.1 Power

Foxconn is replacing a roughly 60-year-old industrial substation it described as inefficient and unreliable. A new substation, transformers, and switchgear are planned west of the existing equipment. Public records do **not** disclose:

- MVA rating;
- voltage;
- utility interconnection capacity;
- construction cost;
- engineering-center load;
- manufacturing peak load;
- backup generation or storage; or
- a dedicated generation source/PPA.

The nearby **940 MW Lordstown Energy Center** and **~950 MW Trumbull Energy Center** are sometimes cited as evidence of “available power.” Proximity does not establish contractual supply, transmission deliverability, or spare capacity. No reviewed document dedicates either plant to Stargate or the engineering center.

### 4.2 Cooling

No site-specific cooling design has been published for the engineering center. Foxconn and OpenAI discuss manufacturing power and cooling systems generally, but that is a product category—not proof that the Lordstown test center uses a particular liquid-cooling topology.

Unknowns include:

- direct-to-chip liquid cooling versus air cooling;
- coolant distribution units and heat-rejection design;
- design supply/return temperatures;
- PUE and WUE;
- heat-reuse plans; and
- emergency cooling during power loss.

### 4.3 Water and wastewater

Foxconn’s broader factory conversion includes a replacement wastewater-treatment plant. Industrial wastewater will be treated onsite to Warren’s acceptance standard before entering the City of Warren system. The plant uses process water for manufacturing, HVAC/cooling towers, and other industrial functions.

Lordstown also discussed a redundant **24-inch water line**, estimated below **$3 million** and to be fully paid by Foxconn. This is valuable plant infrastructure, but it is not proof of a water-intensive hyperscale data center. No record separates engineering-center water from factory process water or gives annual volumes.

## 5. Why a per-GW cost stack is not defensible

### 5.1 No valid Lordstown MW denominator

OpenAI’s 1.5 GW number is explicitly combined across Lordstown and Milam County. Milam’s disclosed lease capacity is 1.2 GW. A mechanical subtraction gives **0.3 GW**, but there are three reasons not to treat it as Lordstown capacity:

1. “Can scale to” numbers may be rounded and non-additive.
2. The timing and boundary of Milam’s 1.2 GW lease may not match the OpenAI announcement.
3. Foxconn later described Lordstown as a small engineering center and denied a full-scale data center at the property.

Therefore Lordstown MW is **unknown**, not 300 MW.

### 5.2 Rejected illustrative scenario

For transparency, if an analyst nevertheless imposed the unsupported 0.3 GW residual:

| Cost item | Total | Artificial 0.3 GW normalization |
|---|---:|---:|
| Asset purchase | $375M | $1.25B/GW |
| Reported retool ceiling | $3.0B | $10.0B/GW |
| Combined | $3.375B | $11.25B/GW |

This table should **not** be used as a Lordstown data-center cost stack. The numerator is mostly acquisition and manufacturing conversion, while the denominator is not disclosed. It fails both boundary tests.

### 5.3 The usable cost stack

The defensible industrial stack is:

| Layer | Public amount | Confidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Land/buildings acquisition | $88M | High—transaction disclosure |
| Existing machinery/equipment acquisition | $287M | High—transaction disclosure |
| Retooling/new investment | Up to $3B | Low-to-medium—reported ceiling, no sources-and-uses |
| Engineering-center compute | Unknown | No separate budget |
| New substation/switchgear | Unknown | Physical work disclosed, cost/rating absent |
| Wastewater plant/water line | Water line reportedly <$3M; treatment plant unknown | Partial |
| Working capital/inventory | Unknown | Material for manufacturing, not disclosed |

The appropriate unit metrics would eventually be cost per rack/module shipped, plant utilization, gross margin, and engineering-center test capacity—not dollars per GW.

## 6. Avoiding three common project conflations

### 6.1 The separate Lordstown/Jackson Township proposal

Municipal records and reporting also discuss a separate large data-center proposal associated with other parcels/developers in the Lordstown/Jackson Township area. Water estimates, multi-building plans, litigation, and moratorium debates tied to that development should not be assigned to the Foxconn/SoftBank Stargate site.

Lordstown’s moratorium did not retroactively erase the already permitted Foxconn engineering center. The April transcript specifically distinguished the projects.

### 6.2 The Pike County / southern Ohio campus

In August 2026, SB Energy, OpenAI, Nvidia, AEP, and government parties announced a much larger AI-power development at the former Portsmouth gaseous-diffusion site in **Pike County, southern Ohio**. Reports describe an initial 800 MW phase and a multi-gigawatt ultimate plan with major grid/generation investment.

That is **not Lordstown**. Its MW, gas generation, grid-upgrade budget, financing guarantees, and Nvidia commitments must not be used to fill Lordstown’s missing fields. The emergence of Pike County arguably reinforces Lordstown’s role as a manufacturing/validation node while hyperscale compute moves to a separate Ohio site.

### 6.3 Nearby power plants

Lordstown Energy Center and Trumbull Energy Center are geographically nearby but not disclosed captive supplies for Stargate. Nameplate proximity is not a power contract.

## 9. Known, inferred, and missing

### Known

- $375M asset transaction.
- ~6.0–6.2M sq ft former auto plant on ~600 acres.
- 50/50 SoftBank–Foxconn manufacturing venture.
- Small permitted engineering data center under site work.
- Factory, substation, wastewater, rail, dock, and stormwater work.
- No purchase obligation in published OpenAI–Foxconn collaboration.
- No full-scale data center at the Foxconn property per April 2026 site representative.

### Reasonably inferred

- The engineering center is a reference/validation environment for factory-built modules.
- Most capital is industrial conversion rather than deployed AI compute.
- Lordstown’s strategic value is schedule compression and domestic supply-chain integration for other Stargate sites.

### Materially missing

- Engineering-center square footage, rack count, MW, capex, chips, and commissioning date.
- Substation MVA, voltage, energization date, and utility agreement.
- Cooling topology, PUE, WUE, annual water, and generator/storage design.
- JV capital contributions, lease terms, governance, and order book.
- Approved retool budget and spending-to-date.
- Production products, unit capacity, customers, revenue, and utilization.
- Current employment and recall status.
- Tax incentives, grants, or abatements tied specifically to the conversion.

## 10. Falsifiers and next records to obtain

The classification should change if SoftBank, Foxconn, OpenAI, or a utility files any of the following:

1. A utility-service/interconnection agreement showing hyperscale Lordstown load.
2. A permit set for multiple production data halls rather than the engineering center.
3. A tenant lease or capacity contract allocating substantial MW to Lordstown.
4. A superseding Foxconn statement announcing full-scale compute on the property.
5. A project-finance package tied to Lordstown MW and commissioning tests.

High-value records include the engineering-center permit drawings, electrical one-line, Trumbull County inspection records, utility capacity letter, Crescent Dune–Foxconn lease/JV agreement, environmental permits, and any local incentive application.

## Assessment

Lordstown matters to Stargate, but not in the way the headline list of “data-center sites” implies. The project is building a **small test environment** and converting one of the country’s largest idle industrial plants into a **domestic factory for data-center systems**. The transaction and retooling may ultimately represent several billion dollars of industrial capital, yet there is no defensible Lordstown GW denominator, no public hyperscale lease, no utility capacity contract, and no disclosed cooling or compute stack.

The analytical error would be to force Lordstown into the same template as Abilene, Saline, Milam, or Port Washington. Its key metrics are factory capex, module throughput, order coverage, lead-time reduction, and engineering-validation capacity. Until a superseding filing establishes production-scale compute, Lordstown should be tracked as **Stargate’s manufacturing and engineering hub—not its last unmeasured gigawatt campus**.

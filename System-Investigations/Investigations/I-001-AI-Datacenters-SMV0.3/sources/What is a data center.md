## Key Takeaways

- The literal servers in the racks running AI training or inference actually consume more power than the average server; it's not just volume/quantity. It's greater power density. 
- AI workloads thus require larger power distribution units that can cope with higher power densities.
- Non-AI loads such as the more typical web hosting, file storage, email servers, anything in the cloud, tend to be lower power density, lower cooling requirements, don't need the specialized power distribution units, etc.
- These significantly different infrastructure needs may contribute to the new construction; better to build something specialized.
- Power requirements are going to be a significant limiter to data center construction and growth; it's generally slower to build, and the demands are high.

## Useful Quotes

>"There are four core components to most data centers: — IT equipment. Data centers host servers, storage devices, and network devices that handle data processing, storage, and transmissions needs. — Infrastructure and utilities. Data centers are equipped with air-conditioning, redundant electricity systems, and electricity conditioning to ensure uninterrupted operations. — Connectivity. Data centers are typically located near high-bandwidth fiber networks that enable low-cost, high-speed data exchange. — Physical security. Robust physical security measures, including fire suppression systems and restricted access, are typically implemented to protect the center’s equipment and data."

>"Ten years ago, a center with 30-megawatt capacity was considered large; today, a 200-megawatt campus is considered normal."

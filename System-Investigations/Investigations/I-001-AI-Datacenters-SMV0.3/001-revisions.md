# 001 Model Revisions

## Revision 0.1 — 2026-09-09

### What changed

The 0.0 model described AI datacenter construction as a direct conversion of capital, land, electricity and semiconductors into rentable compute, with primary owners making a broad bet on continuing AI demand. Version 0.1 replaces that sketch with a layered system model covering:

- the physical stack from powered land through commissioned compute;
- the contractual chain that makes construction financeable;
- separate ownership and financing of campus, power and compute assets;
- project stages from announcement through utilization;
- capital, information, material, energy, compute and revenue flows;
- the current per-GW capital and recurring-cost observations;
- bottlenecks, feedback loops, risk allocation and duration mismatch; and
- explicit beliefs, confidence levels, limits and unresolved questions.

### Why

The site and capital-stack research falsified the implicit idea that a single primary investor usually funds and owns an AI datacenter from end to end. The recurring structure is a chain: an anchor compute commitment supports a cloud-provider or direct lease; the lease supports landlord project debt and equity; separate tariffs or energy contracts support utility assets; and the tenant or another equipment owner finances the shorter-lived compute layer. Risk is transferred among balance sheets but remains dependent on future utilization and AI product revenue.

### Confidence

**High** in the qualitative structure: ownership is layered, long-term contracts unlock capital, power is a central physical constraint, and productive capacity requires the full coupled system.

**Medium** in the quantitative benchmark of approximately $43B-$52B per initially equipped published GW. The independent observations are directionally consistent, but site boundaries and GW denominators differ, most equipment budgets are private, and the sample is concentrated in unusually large Stargate-linked projects.

**Low to medium** in any forecast that current commitments will earn an adequate return. Revenue, utilization, lease pricing, hardware refresh and much of the operating-cost stack remain undisclosed.

### What would reverse or materially change this revision?

- Evidence that most campus financing is not actually supported by long-term tenant or compute commitments.
- Full sources-and-uses schedules showing that the inferred powered-campus or compute layers substantially overlap or are misclassified.
- Comparable projects establishing a much lower or higher fully equipped cost per critical IT GW.
- Evidence that hardware remains economically productive for nearly as long as the building and financing, removing the duration mismatch.
- A durable, diversified re-leasing market showing that specialized AI campuses retain value largely independent of the original tenant.
- Utilization and unit-economics disclosures demonstrating either that current capacity comfortably self-funds or that the demand chain cannot support its fixed obligations.
